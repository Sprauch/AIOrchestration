# LinkedIn post — "What running an AI team actually looks like"

I spent the last week building and running an AI engineering team: five agents (PM, Designer, Tech Lead, Developers, Reviewer) working on a real codebase with structured handoffs, safety gates, and a live dashboard, because I wanted to understand what agentic AI actually changes in practice, not just in theory. They created 24 branches. Four were useful. The waste wasn't an AI problem. It was a leadership problem. Missing product briefs, loose scoping, handoffs I'd left vague because I expected the agents to sort things out themselves. They did sort things out, just in the wrong direction, confidently and at speed. What made the difference wasn't better models. It was the same stuff that always has: clear priorities, tight contracts between roles, knowing when to stop, and being honest about which failures are yours. The experiment pulled on everything I've built over the years across engineering, team leadership, and product thinking. Good outcomes don't come from raw capability alone.
