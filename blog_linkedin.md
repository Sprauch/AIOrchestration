# LinkedIn post — "What running an AI team actually looks like"

I spent the last week building and running an AI engineering team: five agents (PM, Designer, Tech Lead, Developers, Reviewer) working on a real codebase with structured handoffs, safety gates, and a live dashboard, because I wanted to understand what agentic AI actually changes in practice, not just in theory. They created 24 branches. Four were useful. The waste wasn't an AI problem. I'd left handoffs vague, skipped the product brief, and expected the agents to sort things out themselves. They did sort things out, just in the wrong direction, confidently and at speed. Better models wouldn't have fixed any of that. Clear priorities would have. Tight contracts between roles would have. Knowing when to stop would have. The experiment pulled on everything I've built over the years across engineering, team leadership, and product thinking. Turns out raw capability without clarity, constraints, and honest direction just produces confident waste faster. AI makes that tradeoff very visible.
