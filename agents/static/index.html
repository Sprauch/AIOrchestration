The specification, as given

Recorded 2026-09-21, in the user's own words. The calculations behind these numbers are to be defined later; the structure is fixed now so the retrospective role has somewhere to put its output.

  1. Overview — list threads that have been reviewed along with their respective impact (ordered by size of impact), and show any threads currently under review.
  2. Impact — show for each thread reviewed the impact found, with the ability to drill down in what context it happened in.
  3. Root Cause — list Root Causes identified per thread, along with assigned impact value per root cause. Provide an overview of Root Cause Areas (bundled Root Causes), as well as the frequency of recurrence of these root cause areas over time, and the total cost impact they've had.
  4. Improvements — list improvements intended to address individual root causes, ordered by size of total impact of the root cause area the root causes belong to.
The key purpose

“The key purpose of the retro agent and the information it generates is to be able to create proposals that address these root cause areas based on the risk they present over time vs the cost effort of making the change and the risk associated with the change itself, rather than immediately fixing things without understanding that balance of risk vs value.”

Impact found, per thread

The impact found for each reviewed thread, and the context it happened in: what was being attempted, what it cost, and what made it cost that.

Root causes, and the areas they group into

Root causes identified per thread with an impact value assigned to each, then grouped into root cause areas. An area carries three numbers: how often it recurs, over what period, and what it has cost in total.

The grouping is the point. One thread's root cause is an anecdote; the same cause appearing across six threads over two months is a case for changing something structural.

Improvements worth proposing

Improvements intended to address individual root causes, ordered by the total impact of the area the cause belongs to rather than by the impact of any single incident.

This ordering is deliberate: the loudest recent failure is rarely the most expensive one. An improvement here is a candidate for a proposal, weighed on risk over time against cost and change risk — not a queue of fixes.
